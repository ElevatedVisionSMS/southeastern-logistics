Container Drayage

Southeastern Logistics moves containers between South Florida ports, rail ramps, warehouses, and customers.

What is container drayage?

Drayage is the short-distance truck move of a container between a port or rail ramp and a warehouse, distribution center, or customer. It includes pickup, delivery, and return of the empty container.

Which South Florida terminals do you serve?
  • PortMiami: POMTOC, Seaboard Marine, King Ocean, Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd, CMA CGM, and MSC
  • Port Everglades: South Florida Container Terminal, APM Terminals, Crowley, and FIT
  • Port of Palm Beach: Tropical Shipping
  • Rail: Florida East Coast Railway ramps in Miami and Fort Lauderdale
How does a drayage move work from pickup to delivery?

A driver picks up the container at the terminal, delivers it to the consignee, and returns the empty to the designated depot. Each step may require an appointment or gate time.

What affects drayage timing?

Terminal congestion, chassis availability, appointment availability, and delivery-site hours all affect timing. Early scheduling reduces the impact.

LTL & FTL Freight

We move freight by less-than-truckload (LTL) and full truckload (FTL), on routes from Miami up the East Coast.

What is the difference between LTL and FTL?

LTL (less-than-truckload) shares trailer space with other shipments and is priced by weight, freight class, and distance. FTL (full truckload) gives one customer a dedicated trailer.

How is LTL pricing calculated?

Pricing considers weight, freight class, distance, and accessorials such as liftgate or appointment fees.

What areas do your LTL and FTL routes cover?

From Miami up the East Coast to New York and New Jersey.

Last-Mile Delivery

Our last-mile service covers the final leg from a regional hub, port, or warehouse to a commercial delivery address.

What counts as last-mile delivery?

Last-mile delivery is the final leg from a regional hub, port, or warehouse to the delivery address.

Do you offer appointment-based delivery?

Yes. Book delivery appointments at least 48 hours ahead.

Can you deliver to residential or hard-to-reach addresses?

No. We deliver to commercial addresses only.

Temperature-Controlled Transport

We move refrigerated and perishable cargo. The questions below cover reefer drayage, gensets, temperature monitoring, and common delays.

What is reefer drayage?

Reefer drayage is the truck move of a refrigerated container between a port or rail ramp and a cold storage facility, warehouse, or customer, with temperature control maintained throughout.

What is a genset, and when is it used?

A genset is a portable generator that powers a reefer container when it is not connected to terminal or vessel power, such as during a truck move.

What causes reefer delays?

Common causes include:

  • Delivery appointment problems
  • Documentation holds
  • Weather

What permits does overweight transport need in Florida?

Moves over legal size or weight limits need a permit from the Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) State Permit Office.
